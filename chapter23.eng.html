Module 23: Accident Response & First Aid in Industry

When an accident occurs, the first actions must be quick but also safe. The aim is to protect ourselves, activate help without delay, support the injured person and prevent a second injury.

Learning Objectives

🛑 Apply the correct sequence of actions when an accident occurs.
📞 Recognise when immediate emergency assistance is required.
🩹 Understand basic actions for common industrial injuries.
❤️ Understand the role of CPR and an AED during an emergency.
📋 Understand the importance of reporting, investigation and learning after an incident.

23.1 The Response Model: Protect → Alert → Assess → Assist → Report

When an accident occurs, the first instinct may be to run immediately towards the injured person. In an industrial environment, however, the hazard may still be active.

1
PROTECT First check whether you can approach without putting yourself at risk.
2
ALERT Call for help and activate the emergency response procedure.
3
ASSESS Check the condition of the injured person and look for immediately life-threatening conditions.
4
ASSIST Provide appropriate first aid according to your training.
5
REPORT After the incident, reporting and investigation follow.
Core rule: Do not become a second casualty. Assistance begins only when the approach can be made safely.

23.2 Before You Approach: Is the Hazard Still Active?

In an industrial workplace, the accident may have already happened, but the source of the hazard may still be active.

Electrical Energy ⚡

Electrical Incident

Do not touch the injured person until it has been confirmed that they are no longer in contact with an energised electrical source.

Mechanical Energy ⚙️

Machinery

Check for moving parts, stored energy or the possibility of unexpected movement.

Chemical Exposure 🧪

Spill or Vapour

Do not enter an area with an unknown chemical exposure without proper hazard control and an appropriate response.

Fire 🔥

Heat / Smoke

Do not enter an area involving fire, dense smoke or another uncontrolled hazard.

Traffic 🚜

Vehicles & Mobile Equipment

Make sure the area is protected from vehicles, forklifts or other moving equipment.

23.3 Initial Assessment of the Injured Person

🗣️

Responsiveness

Check whether the person responds and call for immediate help when required.

🫁

Breathing

If the person is unresponsive and is not breathing normally, this is a situation requiring immediate emergency response.

🩸

Severe Bleeding

Look for heavy or uncontrolled bleeding that requires immediate attention.

Priority: In a serious incident, do not delay activating emergency assistance.

23.4 Severe Bleeding

Severe external bleeding can quickly develop into a life-threatening emergency.

📞

Call for Help

Immediately activate the required emergency response.

🧤

Protect Yourself

When available, use an appropriate protective barrier to reduce contact with blood.

🩹

Direct Pressure

Apply firm, direct pressure to the bleeding area using an appropriate dressing or material, in accordance with your training.

Severe or uncontrolled bleeding = emergency. Monitor the injured person until appropriate personnel take over.

23.5 Burns

🔥

Stop the Exposure

Remove the person from the source of the burn only when this can be done safely.

💧

Cool a Thermal Burn

For a thermal burn, cool the affected area with cool running water in accordance with your training and first-aid procedure.

🚫

Do Not Improvise

Do not apply ice, creams, oil or other unapproved substances to the burn.

Note: Chemical and electrical burns require additional precautions.

23.6 Chemical Exposure as a First-Aid Incident

Chemical safety has already been covered in a previous module. Here we focus only on the response after exposure has already occurred.

👁️

Chemical in the Eyes

Begin flushing immediately using the designated eyewash station and follow the product-specific instructions and site procedure.

✋

Chemical on the Skin

Stop the exposure and follow the required procedure for removing the product and contaminated clothing.

🫁

Inhalation

Do not enter a hazardous atmosphere without the required protection. Activate appropriate assistance immediately.

👄

Ingestion

Do not induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by an appropriate medical source or by the product instructions.

There is no single first-aid response for every chemical. Use the first-aid information for the specific product and follow the site's emergency procedure.

23.7 Electrical Accident

⚡

Do Not Touch the Injured Person

If they may still be in contact with electrical current, do not touch them.

🔌

Isolate the Hazard

The electrical source must be placed in a safe condition before approaching.

❤️

Monitor the Person

Electrical injury can affect the heart and breathing. Activate emergency assistance and monitor the person's condition.

23.8 Other Common Industrial Injuries

👁️

Eye Injury

Do not rub the eye. If an object has penetrated the eye, do not attempt to remove it.

🦴

Possible Fracture

Do not attempt to straighten or reposition an injured limb.

⬇️

Serious Fall

Avoid unnecessary movement of a seriously injured person, unless there is an immediate danger at the scene.

23.9 Unresponsiveness, CPR & AED

1

Check Responsiveness

If the person does not respond, call for help immediately.

2

Check Breathing

If they are not breathing normally, activate the emergency response without delay.

3

CPR

Begin CPR according to your training or the instructions provided by the emergency service.

4

AED

Ask for an AED, switch it on and follow the device's voice and visual instructions.

Time is critical. Rapid recognition, calling for help, CPR and early use of an AED are key elements of emergency response.

23.10 After the Immediate Response

The response does not end when the injured person leaves or when the area has been made safe.

📢

Incident Reporting

The incident should be reported according to the organisation's procedure.

🔍

Investigation

The facts, conditions and contributing factors are examined.

🛠️

Corrective Actions

Findings are converted into measures that reduce the likelihood of recurrence.

📚

Organisational Learning

Accidents and near misses are used to improve prevention and preparedness.

Purpose: Learn from the incident and reduce the likelihood of it happening again.

Key Takeaways

🛑
PROTECT: first check that the area is safe.
📞
ALERT: in a serious incident, activate assistance immediately.
👀
ASSESS: check responsiveness, breathing and severe bleeding.
🩹
ASSIST: provide appropriate first aid according to your training.
📋
REPORT: the incident should lead to investigation, learning and prevention.
