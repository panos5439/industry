Module 8: Manual Handling

Manual handling is a common task in industrial workplaces, but poor technique can cause injuries to the back, shoulders, arms, hands and knees.

Learning Objectives

📦 Recognise hazards associated with manual handling.
🧍 Apply basic principles of safe body posture.
🛠️ Use mechanical or handling aids where required.
✅ Avoid lifting when the load cannot be handled safely.

What is Manual Handling?

Manual handling includes any task in which a worker lifts, carries, pushes, pulls, holds or positions a load using physical effort.

Important: Weight is not the only factor. Risk is also affected by body posture, carrying distance, frequency, poor grip, the working environment and time pressure.

When Does a Load Become Hazardous?

🏋️

It is Too Heavy

When excessive force is required or the load cannot be controlled safely.

📐

It Has an Awkward Shape

Large, unstable, sharp or without suitable handholds.

↔️

It is Held Away from the Body

The farther the load is from the body, the greater the mechanical stress on the lower back.

🔁

The Task is Repetitive

Frequent repetition increases fatigue and the risk of musculoskeletal injury.

🚧

The Work Area is Unsafe

Slippery floors, obstacles, poor lighting or restricted access increase the risk.

⏱️

There is Time Pressure

Rushing can lead to poor posture and unsafe decisions.

Basic Safe Lifting Technique

1. Think Before You Lift

Check the load, the route, the destination and whether assistance is required.

2. Get Close to the Load

Keep the load as close to your body as possible.

3. Use a Stable Base

Position your feet securely so that you maintain good balance.

4. Bend the Knees

Use your legs and keep your back as close as possible to a neutral position.

5. Avoid Twisting

If you need to change direction, move your feet and turn your whole body.

6. Set the Load Down in a Controlled Way

Place the load down using a controlled movement.

Do not try to prove your strength. If the load is heavy, unstable or awkward, ask for assistance or use suitable mechanical handling equipment.

Use of Handling Aids

Often the safest solution is not to lift better, but to avoid unnecessary manual lifting altogether.

🛒

Trolleys

For moving boxes, tools or materials over longer distances.

🏗️

Lifting Equipment

Hoists, overhead cranes, lifting tables or forklifts.

🤝

Team Lifting

When the load is large or unstable, coordination and clear communication are required.

📦

Reduce the Load

Split the load into smaller parts where possible.

Key Takeaways

🧠
Think before you lift.
📦
Keep the load close to your body.
↩️
Avoid twisting your back while carrying a load.
🛒
Use handling aids when the load is heavy, awkward or unstable.
🛡️
The safest option is often to avoid unnecessary manual lifting altogether.
