Module 21: Working with a Computer

Good workstation ergonomics helps reduce musculoskeletal strain, eye fatigue and overall physical discomfort.

Learning Objectives

🖥️ Recognise basic ergonomic hazards when working with a computer.
🪑 Adjust the chair, screen and equipment correctly.
👁️ Apply good practices to reduce eye fatigue.
⏱️ Include short breaks and changes of posture during the working day.

21.1 Risks from Prolonged Computer Work

Prolonged screen work, particularly when the workstation is not properly adjusted, can lead to physical and visual strain.

🦴

Musculoskeletal Strain

Discomfort in the neck, shoulders, lower back, wrists and hands.

👀

Eye Fatigue

Dry eyes, blurred vision, discomfort or headaches.

🧠

Mental Fatigue

Reduced concentration, tiredness and increased stress.

🖐️

Repetitive Movements

Continuous use of a keyboard and mouse may increase strain on the hands and wrists.

Key message: Ergonomics is not only about posture. It involves adapting the entire workstation to the needs of the user.

21.2 Workstation Setup

Observe the image and use the following principles to organise your workstation correctly.

🖥️

Screen Position

The screen should be positioned directly in front of the user and allow a comfortable head and neck position.

Viewing distance is generally around an arm's length, depending on screen size and the individual user's needs.

🪑

Chair & Body Position

The back should be supported, the shoulders should remain relaxed and the feet should be firmly supported on the floor or on a footrest.

⌨️

Keyboard & Mouse

Keep them close to the body so that the elbows remain in a comfortable position and the wrists remain as neutral as possible.

Interactive Activity: Adjust the Eye Level

Use the buttons to move the eye-level line until it is approximately aligned with the upper part of the screen.

Eye Level Line
Move the line until you find the correct position.

21.3 Working Time and Breaks

👁️

20-20-20 Rule

Periodically look away from the screen. A commonly used practice is the 20-20-20 rule: about every 20 minutes, look for approximately 20 seconds at something around 6 metres away.

🚶

Short Breaks

Change position, stand up, walk a little and perform gentle movements when the task allows.

🔄

Change of Activity

Where possible, alternate tasks to avoid prolonged static posture.

21.4 Lighting and Work Environment

💡

Lighting

Lighting should be adequate, without excessive glare or distracting reflections on the screen.

🪟

Natural Light

The workstation should be positioned to reduce reflections and strong differences in brightness.

🌬️

Comfortable Environment

Temperature, ventilation and air quality should support comfortable work.

21.5 Practical Tips

🔆 Adjust screen brightness to a comfortable level for your eyes.
🧍 Avoid prolonged hunched or twisted postures.
🎧 Use an appropriate headset when frequent telephone use is required.
↕️ Change your working posture where this is reasonably practicable.
Remember: There is no single posture that should remain exactly the same all day. Correct adjustment, comfortable posture and regular changes of position work together.

Key Takeaways

🖥️
Adjust the screen so that you can work with a comfortable head and neck position.
🪑
Use appropriate support and keep your feet stable.
⌨️
The keyboard and mouse should be within easy reach without excessive stretching.
👁️
Take regular visual breaks and reduce reflections and glare.
🚶
Avoid remaining still for long periods. Change posture and move when practicable.
