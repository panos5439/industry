Module 10: Chemicals & Safe Handling

Chemicals are used in many industrial processes, maintenance activities, cleaning operations and production tasks. Safe use begins with correctly identifying the product and understanding its hazards.

Learning Objectives

🧪 Recognise key chemical hazards in an industrial workplace.
🏷️ Understand the importance of labels and Safety Data Sheets.
🦺 Identify the required protective measures and PPE.
🚨 Respond appropriately to a spill, exposure or other chemical incident.

10.1 Where Do We Encounter Chemicals in Industry?

Chemicals may be used in many different activities throughout an industrial facility.

🧽

Cleaning & Degreasing

Cleaners, degreasers, disinfectants and solvents.

🛢️

Oils & Lubricants

Machine lubricants, hydraulic fluids and industrial oils.

🎨

Paints & Solvents

Paints, thinners, adhesives and other coating materials.

🧯

Gases & Cylinders

Oxygen, nitrogen, propane and other gases under pressure.

🧪

Acids & Alkalis

Corrosive products used in cleaning or production processes.

🔧

Maintenance

Sprays, cleaners, adhesives, lubricants and specialised maintenance chemicals.

Key message: A substance should not be considered safe simply because it is used every day or because only a small amount is present. First identify the product and read the hazard information.

10.2 Main Hazards from Chemicals

👁️

Eye or Skin Contact

Contact may cause irritation, burns or serious injury.

🫁

Inhalation

Vapours, gases, mists or dusts may affect the respiratory system.

🔥

Fire / Explosion

Some substances may ignite from heat, a spark or a flame.

⚗️

Incompatible Chemicals

Mixing incompatible products may cause a hazardous reaction.

🧴

Incorrect Transfer

A chemical without the correct label can result in misuse or serious exposure.

🌊

Environmental Pollution

Spills or incorrect disposal can affect drains, soil and the aquatic environment.

10.3 Before Using a Chemical

1

Identify the Product

Make sure you know exactly which product you are using and what task it is intended for.

2

Read the Label

Check the pictograms, warnings and key safety instructions.

3

Check the SDS

Use the Safety Data Sheet for information about hazards, protection, storage and emergency actions.

4

Check Protective Measures

Make sure the required PPE, ventilation and safe working conditions are available.

Never use an unknown product. If a container has no label or you cannot confirm what it contains, do not use it.

10.4 Chemical Hazard Pictograms (GHS)

The following are the same pictograms used in the original training material. A pictogram provides a hazard warning, but the complete assessment must be based on the label and the SDS for the specific product.

Acute Toxicity

Examples: toxic pesticides, hydrogen sulfide, some strong solvents.

  • Risk of severe poisoning through inhalation, swallowing or contact.
Flammable

Examples: alcohols, gasoline, aerosols, solvents.

  • May ignite from heat, flame or a spark.
Oxidising

Examples: hydrogen peroxide, nitrates, some bleaching products.

  • May intensify combustion or cause a violent reaction.
Explosive

Examples: explosive mixtures, pyrotechnic materials, unstable organic peroxides.

  • Risk of explosion from heat, impact, friction or flame.
Corrosive

Examples: hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid, strong cleaning products.

  • May cause severe skin burns, serious eye damage and corrosion of metals.
Irritant / Harmful

Examples: detergents, adhesives, mild cleaning products.

  • May cause skin, eye or respiratory irritation.
Serious Health Hazard

Examples: carcinogenic or mutagenic substances.

  • May be associated with serious long-term health effects.
Hazardous to the Environment

Examples: pesticides, oils and industrial chemicals.

  • May cause water pollution and harm to organisms.
Gas Under Pressure

Examples: oxygen, propane and compressed nitrogen.

  • Hazard from high pressure, heating or violent pressure release.
Never remove hazard labels. Pictograms, hazard statements and label instructions provide essential safety information.

10.5 Safe Storage and Transfer

🏷️

Keep the Label

The product must remain identifiable throughout its use.

🧴

Use a Suitable Container

Do not transfer chemicals into water bottles, soft-drink bottles or other unrelated containers.

⚗️

Separate Incompatible Materials

Products that may react with each other must be stored according to their requirements.

🌡️

Suitable Conditions

Follow the instructions for temperature, ventilation and ignition sources.

Practical rule: If you cannot positively identify the contents of a container, do not use it.

10.6 In Case of a Spill or Exposure

1

Stop and Assess

Do not approach or clean up an unknown spill without understanding the hazard.

2

Warn Others

Protect nearby workers and restrict access where this can be done safely.

3

Follow the SDS

Use the instructions for spills, exposure, PPE and initial response actions.

4

Inform the Responsible Person

Follow the facility's emergency response or incident-reporting procedure.

Important: Not every spill should be handled in the same way. The appropriate action depends on the specific product, the hazard, the quantity and the instructions in the SDS and the facility procedure.

Key Takeaways

🏷️
Identify the product and read the label before using it.
📄
The SDS provides critical information about hazards and protective measures.
🧴
Never place chemicals in water bottles or other unsuitable, unlabelled containers.
⚗️
Do not mix or store incompatible products without knowing their requirements.
🚨
In case of a spill or exposure, do not improvise: follow the SDS and the facility procedure.
