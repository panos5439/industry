Module 18: Driving Safety

Learn how journey planning, vehicle checks, defensive driving and safe decision-making can prevent road incidents and protect drivers, passengers and other road users.

Learning Objectives

🚗

Identify Driving Hazards

Recognise the main hazards associated with occupational driving and vehicle movements.

🗺️

Plan Journeys Safely

Consider the route, weather, traffic, fatigue and vehicle condition before travelling.

🛡️

Apply Defensive Driving

Maintain a safe speed, following distance and continuous observation of the road environment.

🛑

Make Safe Decisions

Stop or postpone driving when the driver, vehicle or road conditions are not safe.

Why is Driving Safety Important?

Driving for work may appear routine, but it combines the condition of the driver, vehicle, road, weather and traffic environment.

Why first: A road traffic incident can cause fatal injury, permanent disability, property damage and major operational disruption.
Important: No delivery, deadline, meeting or production target is more important than driving safely.

Key Driving Hazards

😴 Human Factor

Fatigue

Fatigue reduces concentration, judgement, reaction time and awareness of the surroundings.

📱 Distraction

Mobile Devices

Calls, messages and navigation adjustments take attention away from the driving task.

🏎️ Speed

Inappropriate Speed

Excessive speed reduces the available reaction time and increases the severity of a collision.

🌧️ Environment

Adverse Conditions

Rain, fog, wind, ice or glare can reduce traction, visibility or vehicle stability.

🔧 Vehicle

Vehicle Defects

Defects involving tyres, brakes, lights or steering can affect safe vehicle operation.

🚶 Road Users

Pedestrians and Cyclists

Vulnerable road users may be difficult to see and can move unexpectedly.

Safe Journey Planning

Safe driving starts before the vehicle moves.

1. Confirm the Need to Travel

Confirm that the journey is necessary and consider whether a safer alternative is available.

2. Select the Route

Consider road type, restrictions and known hazards.

3. Check Conditions

Check traffic, weather, road closures and expected visibility.

4. Plan Time and Breaks

Allow sufficient time to avoid rushing and include appropriate rest breaks.

5. Confirm Driver Fitness

Make sure the driver is rested and fit to drive safely.

6. Prepare for Emergencies

Know the breakdown and emergency response arrangements.

Driver Fitness

😴

Fatigue

Do not drive when concentration or alertness has significantly reduced.

💊

Medication

Check whether medication can cause drowsiness, slower reactions or impaired judgement.

🍺

Alcohol and Drugs

Never drive while impaired by alcohol, illegal drugs or other substances that affect driving ability.

🤒

Illness and Severe Stress

Illness, severe stress or emotional distress can reduce safe driving performance.

If you are not fit to drive safely, do not start or continue the journey.

Pre-Drive Vehicle Check

External Check

  • Tyres appear correctly inflated and free from visible damage.
  • Lights and indicators are working.
  • Windows and mirrors are clean.
  • There are no obvious leaks or loose components.
  • Doors and loads are properly secured.
  • The area around the vehicle is clear.

Cab and Control Check

  • Seat and mirrors are correctly adjusted.
  • Seat belts are available and functional.
  • Brakes and steering operate normally.
  • Wipers and washers operate correctly.
  • Loose objects are secured.
  • Required emergency equipment is available.
Report defects immediately. Do not use a vehicle when a defect could affect its safe operation.

Speed and Safe Following Distance

The posted speed limit is the maximum permitted speed under suitable conditions. A safe speed may need to be lower when visibility, traction, traffic or road conditions are poor.

↔️

Maintain Distance

Keep enough distance to react and stop safely without colliding with the vehicle ahead.

🌧️

Increase Distance

Increase the following distance in rain, fog, darkness or heavy traffic.

👀

Look Well Ahead

Continuously scan for vehicles, pedestrians, signals and road hazards.

🛑

React Early

Early observation reduces the need for sudden braking and emergency manoeuvres.

Distraction-Free Driving

Reading or sending messages, using a handheld phone, adjusting equipment or any other activity that takes attention away from the road can lead to a serious incident.
📵

Mobile Phones

Set up navigation and communication before starting the journey.

🅿️

Stop Safely

Park in a safe location before using a mobile device or adjusting equipment.

🍔

Other Distractions

Avoid eating, reaching for objects or performing tasks that reduce vehicle control.

🧠

Mental Focus

Strong emotions and complex conversations can also reduce attention while driving.

Driving in Adverse Conditions

Condition Main Hazards Safe Response
Heavy rain Reduced traction, visibility and increased stopping distance Reduce speed and increase following distance
Fog Very limited visibility and difficult distance judgement Reduce speed and use appropriate lighting
Ice or snow Loss of steering and braking control Avoid unnecessary travel and use very smooth controls
Strong wind Vehicle instability and sudden lateral movement Reduce speed and maintain increased awareness
Glare or darkness Reduced ability to identify hazards Reduce speed and use appropriate lighting
Extreme heat Driver fatigue, tyre stress and overheating Check the vehicle, stay hydrated and take appropriate breaks

Reversing and Low-Speed Manoeuvres

👀

Check the Area

Check for pedestrians, obstacles and blind spots before manoeuvring.

↩️

Reverse Slowly

Use a controlled speed and stop immediately if visibility is lost.

🦺

Use a Trained Banksman

Use a trained banksman where required and maintain clear agreed signals.

🛑

Stop if Unsure

Never continue a manoeuvre when there is uncertainty about the area around the vehicle.

Warning Signs of Fatigue

🥱

Frequent Yawning

Repeated yawning or heavy eyelids may indicate reduced alertness.

🧠

Reduced Concentration

Missing signs, exits or being unable to remember recent parts of the journey is a serious warning sign.

↔️

Lane Drifting

Drifting, unstable speed or sudden corrections may indicate fatigue.

😠

Irritability

Increased frustration and poor judgement can accompany fatigue.

Opening a window, increasing music volume or consuming caffeine are not reliable substitutes for stopping safely and resting.

Actions After an Incident or Breakdown

🛑

Stop Safely

Move away from the traffic flow where possible and avoid creating an additional hazard.

🚨

Warn Others

Use hazard warning lights and approved warning equipment where safe and required.

📞

Call for Assistance

Contact emergency services, roadside assistance or the company as appropriate.

📋

Report the Event

Follow the required process for reporting incidents, damage or near misses.

Do not attempt roadside repairs when passing traffic, poor visibility or the vehicle position creates an unacceptable risk.

Key Takeaways

🗺️
Safe driving starts before the vehicle moves.
📵
Mobile devices and other distractions must not take attention away from the road.
🛣️
Speed and following distance must be adjusted to the actual road and weather conditions.
😴
Fatigue is a serious hazard. Stop safely and rest when warning signs appear.
🛑
No deadline is more important than arriving safely.
