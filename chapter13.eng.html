Module 13: Working at Height

Working at height can result in serious or fatal injuries. Fall prevention must be planned before the work begins.

Learning Objectives

🧗 Understand what is considered working at height.
⬇️ Recognise the risks of people and objects falling.
🪜 Use ladders and work platforms safely.
🛡️ Inspect and use suitable fall protection equipment.

What is Working at Height?

Working at height is any work where there is a possibility of falling from one level to another, even when the height appears relatively small. It may include work on ladders, platforms, scaffolds, roofs, tanks, vehicles or machinery.

Key message: Height alone does not determine the level of risk. The work surface, duration, access method, tools, weather conditions and the possibility of falling objects must also be considered.

Main Hazards

⬇️

Worker Falling

Falls from ladders, platforms, floor edges, openings or scaffolds.

📦

Falling Objects

Tools or materials can fall and injure people working below.

🪜

Unsuitable Ladder

Damaged, incorrectly positioned or used for the wrong type of task.

🌧️

Environmental Conditions

Wind, rain, slippery surfaces or poor lighting.

🔧

Unsafe Tool Handling

Carrying tools by hand instead of using a suitable tool pouch or tether.

🚫

Missing Protection

Missing guardrails, covers, harnesses or suitable anchor points.

Hierarchy of Control for Working at Height

1. Avoid Working at Height

If the task can be completed from ground level, this is usually the safest option.

2. Prevent the Fall

Use suitable platforms, guardrails, scaffolding or other safe access systems.

3. Minimise the Consequences

Where the risk cannot be avoided, use a suitable work-restraint or fall-arrest system.

Important: A safety harness is not enough on its own. A suitable anchor point, compatible lanyard, equipment inspection and a rescue plan are also required.

Safe Use of Ladders

🔍

Inspect Before Use

Check the rungs, feet, stability, condition and cleanliness.

📐

Correct Positioning

The ladder must stand on a stable surface and be positioned correctly.

🤲

Maintain Three Points of Contact

Keep two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand, in contact with the ladder.

🚫

Do Not Overreach

If you cannot reach the work area, climb down and reposition the ladder.

Falling Objects

Working at height does not only create risk for the person working above. People at lower levels may also be exposed to serious injury from falling tools or materials.

Safe practice: Use suitable tool pouches, tool tethers and a clearly defined exclusion zone beneath the work area.

Key Takeaways

Work at height must be planned before it begins.

First consider whether working at height can be avoided.

A ladder is not always the correct solution.

Fall protection requires suitable equipment, anchorage and a rescue plan.

Protect people working below the elevated work area.
