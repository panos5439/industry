Module 20: Industrial Hygiene

Industrial Hygiene focuses on identifying, assessing and preventing worker exposure to physical and chemical agents such as noise, dust, vibration and heat stress.

Learning Objectives

🔊

Noise

🌫️

Dust & Airborne Particles

Understand how airborne contaminants are generated and how they may enter the body.

🫨

Vibration

Recognise hazards associated with hand tools and mechanical equipment.

🌡️

Heat Stress

Recognise warning signs and effective preventive measures.

Why is Industrial Hygiene Important?

Occupational exposure does not always result in an immediate injury. Health effects may develop gradually after repeated or long-term exposure.

Why first: Hearing loss, respiratory problems, musculoskeletal disorders and heat-related illness can develop without an obvious accident.
Key principle: We do not rely only on Personal Protective Equipment. Priority should be given to elimination, substitution and engineering controls at the source of exposure.

Main Industrial Hygiene Hazards

🔊 Noise

High Noise Levels

Presses, machinery, tools and production equipment can generate harmful levels of noise.

🌫️ Particles

Dust and Fumes

Grinding, cutting, material handling and casting operations can generate airborne contaminants.

🫨 Vibration

Hand-Arm or Whole-Body Vibration

Tools, machinery or vehicles can transfer vibration to the worker.

🌡️ Heat

Heat Stress

Furnaces, foundries, hot processes and high ambient temperatures increase the worker's thermal load.

Hierarchy of Controls

1. Elimination

Remove the hazard source where reasonably practicable.

2. Substitution

Use a safer process or a less hazardous material.

3. Engineering Controls

Use enclosure, extraction, ventilation and isolation of the hazard source.

4. Administrative Controls

Limit exposure time, organise work appropriately and rotate tasks where suitable.

5. PPE

Use suitable personal protective equipment where the risk cannot be adequately controlled by other means.

Noise

Prolonged exposure to high noise levels can cause permanent hearing loss.

🔧

Control at Source

Maintain equipment, replace noisy processes where possible and use suitable acoustic enclosures.

🚪

Isolation

Restrict access or physically separate high-noise areas.

⏱️

Exposure Time

Limit the duration of exposure in high-noise areas.

🎧

Hearing Protection

Use suitable earmuffs or earplugs where required.

Dust and Airborne Particles

Visible dust is only part of the problem. Very small particles may remain suspended in the air and enter the respiratory system.

🌬️

Local Exhaust Ventilation

Capture dust and contaminants as close to the source as possible.

💧

Wet Methods

Where suitable, wet methods can help reduce the re-suspension of particles.

🧹

Housekeeping

Prevent the accumulation and re-suspension of dust in work areas.

😷

Respiratory Protection

Use only suitable respiratory protective equipment in accordance with the risk assessment.

Vibration

🛠️

Suitable Tools

Select equipment with lower vibration levels where reasonably practicable.

🔧

Maintenance

Worn components can significantly increase vibration levels.

⏳

Limit Exposure

Control the duration of continuous use of high-vibration tools.

✋

Report Symptoms

Numbness, tingling or pain should be reported at an early stage.

Heat Stress

💧

Hydration

Drink water regularly before severe thirst develops.

🕶️

Rest Breaks

Take planned breaks in a cooler area where required.

🌬️

Ventilation

Use engineering controls to reduce the thermal load.

👀

Monitor Symptoms

Dizziness, weakness, headache or confusion require immediate action.

Serious warning: Confusion, disorientation or loss of consciousness in a hot environment must be treated as an emergency.

When Should Work Stop?

🌫️

Increased Dust

When dust levels increase unusually or ventilation is not operating correctly.

🔊

Noise Control Failure

When an enclosure, barrier or other control measure has been removed or has failed.

🫨

Abnormal Vibration

When a tool or machine develops unusually high vibration.

🌡️

Heat Stress Symptoms

When dizziness, weakness, confusion or other symptoms develop.

Key Takeaways

🔊
Noise should be controlled as far as possible at the source.
🌫️
Dust that cannot be seen can still present a significant health hazard.
🫨
Vibration exposure should be reduced through suitable equipment, maintenance and control of exposure time.
🌡️
Symptoms of heat stress must be recognised and addressed early.
🛡️
Priority should be given to engineering controls rather than relying only on Personal Protective Equipment.
