Module 17: Contractor Safety

Effective contractor safety management requires clear communication, appropriate planning, coordination, adequate supervision and continuous monitoring of actual workplace conditions.

Learning Objectives

👷

Understand Contractor Safety

Understand why contractor activities require planning, communication and supervision.

⚠️

Recognise Hazards

Recognise hazards arising from unfamiliar workplaces, simultaneous activities and changing conditions.

🤝

Shared Responsibilities

Understand the responsibilities of both the host organisation and the contractor.

✅

Follow a Safe Process

Apply the correct process from contractor selection through to safe completion.

Why is Contractor Safety Important?

Contractors often carry out specialised, temporary or high-risk work. They may be working in unfamiliar areas, near operating equipment or alongside other simultaneous activities.

Why it matters: Many incidents occur when hazards, responsibilities, work boundaries or changes are not communicated clearly.
Important: Outsourcing work to a contractor does not remove the need to manage safety. The host organisation and the contractor must cooperate throughout the entire activity.

Who May Be Considered a Contractor?

🔧

Maintenance

Mechanical, electrical, repair and other technical work.

🏗️

Construction

Construction, installation, excavation, demolition and structural work.

🧹

Services

Cleaning, security, waste management and other support services.

🚚

Transport

Drivers, logistics, transport, loading and deliveries.

Common Contractor Work Hazards

🧭 Unfamiliar Environment

Lack of Familiarity

Contractors may not know local hazards, routes or restricted-access areas.

🔄 Interaction

Simultaneous Work

Contractor work may interact with production, maintenance, traffic or other work teams.

🔥 High Risk

High-Risk Activities

Hot work, electrical work, work at height, confined spaces and lifting operations.

🗣️ Communication

Communication Barriers

Language differences, unclear instructions or incorrect assumptions.

🧰 Equipment

Unsuitable Equipment

Damaged, uninspected or unsuitable tools may be brought onto site.

📋 Scope

Uncontrolled Changes

Additional work may be introduced without a new risk assessment or formal approval.

Shared Responsibilities

🏭

Host Organisation

Communicates site hazards, coordinates activities and verifies that controls are in place.

🧑‍💼

Contractor Management

Provides competent personnel, suitable equipment, risk assessments and supervision.

👷

Contractor Supervisor

Briefs the team, monitors compliance and stops unsafe work when necessary.

👥

Contractor Workers

Follow rules, permits and controls, and report hazards and changes.

Contractor Safety Management Process

1. Define the Work

Clearly define the scope, location, duration and requirements.

2. Select the Contractor

Review experience, competence, resources and safety performance.

3. Assess the Risks

Identify hazards and interactions with site activities.

4. Agree the Controls

Permits, isolations, barriers, PPE and procedures.

5. Site Induction

Local rules, hazards and emergency arrangements.

6. Authorise the Work

Verify documents, permits and preconditions.

7. Monitor the Work

Continuously monitor the actual work and any changes.

8. Complete the Work

Safe handover, restoration and review.

Contractor Site Induction

Site Information

  • Hazards and restricted-access areas.
  • Alarms and evacuation routes.
  • First aid and emergency contacts.
  • Traffic routes.
  • Required PPE.
  • Hazard and incident reporting.

Work Information

  • Approved scope and boundaries.
  • Risk assessment.
  • Safe work method.
  • Permit to Work.
  • LOTO and isolations.
  • Actions when conditions change.
Important: Site induction does not replace task-specific training, risk assessment, supervision or a Permit to Work.

Before Starting Work

Document Check

  • The scope of work is clear.
  • The risk assessment has been reviewed.
  • The work method has been approved.
  • Required permits are valid.
  • Competence has been verified.
  • Emergency arrangements are in place.

Worksite Check

  • The correct equipment has been identified.
  • Required isolations have been applied.
  • Barriers and warning signs are in place.
  • Tools and equipment are suitable.
  • Escape routes are clear.
  • Simultaneous activities have been coordinated.
Do not start work if permits, isolations, competence, controls or responsibilities are unclear or incomplete.

Monitoring Contractor Work

🔍

Inspections

Confirm that the actual work matches the approved method.

🗣️

Communication

Discuss changes, new hazards and work progress.

📊

Evaluation

Monitor compliance, observations and corrective actions.

🛑

Stop Work

Stop work immediately when unsafe conditions or unauthorised changes are identified.

Decision Scenario

A contractor has been authorised to repair a pump. During the work, the contractor decides that removing a nearby machine guard would make access easier. The change is not included in the approved work method.

What should happen?

Simultaneous Work Decision

A contractor starts grinding work near an area where another team is handling flammable material. The two jobs were planned separately, but their interaction was not assessed.

When Should Work Stop?

🔄

Conditions Change

When equipment, the work area or the process changes.

📋

Scope Changes

When work is required outside the approved scope.

⌛

Permit Expires

When a required permit is suspended, cancelled or expires.

🚧

Controls Fail

When isolations, barriers or other controls are missing or ineffective.

🚨

Emergency

When an alarm or emergency response is activated.

❓

Instructions Are Unclear

When responsibilities, hazards or work boundaries are not clear.

Work Completion and Handover

🔍

Inspection

Confirm that the work has been completed and no hazards remain.

🧰

Tools and Materials

Remove tools, waste and temporary equipment.

🚧

Temporary Controls

Remove barriers only when it is safe to do so.

📄

Document Closure

Close permits, record changes and complete the formal handover.

Good practice: The final review should capture lessons learned and improvement opportunities for future contractor activities.

Key Takeaways

Contractor safety requires cooperation between the host organisation and the contractor.

The scope, hazards, controls and responsibilities must be clear before work begins.

Contractors must be competent, briefed and authorised.

Work must stop when conditions, scope or controls change.

The work should only be handed back once the area has been confirmed safe.
