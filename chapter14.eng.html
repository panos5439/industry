Module 14: Confined / Restricted Spaces

Confined spaces may appear harmless, but they can contain fatal atmospheres, oxygen deficiency or hazardous stored energy.

Learning Objectives

🚪 Understand what a confined or restricted space is.
⚠️ Recognise key hazards before entry.
📋 Understand the importance of permits and atmospheric testing.
🚑 Understand why rescue must be planned and not improvised.

What is a Confined / Restricted Space?

A confined or restricted space is an area that is not designed for continuous human occupancy, has limited means of entry or exit, and may contain a hazardous atmosphere or other serious hazards.

🛢️

Tanks

Tanks containing liquids, chemicals, fuels or water.

🌾

Silos

Silos containing dust, raw materials or grain, with a potential engulfment hazard.

🕳️

Pits / Manholes

Manholes, underground spaces, channels or maintenance pits.

🔩

Pipes / Vessels

Large pipes, pressure vessels or enclosed sections of equipment.

Key message: Never enter a confined space “just for a moment” without an approved entry procedure.

Main Hazards

🫁

Oxygen Deficiency

The atmosphere may not contain enough oxygen for safe breathing.

☠️

Toxic Gases

Gases or vapours may cause poisoning, unconsciousness or death.

🔥

Flammable Atmosphere

Vapours or gases may ignite from a spark or hot-work activity.

🌊

Engulfment / Entrapment

Liquids, materials or grain can trap or engulf a worker.

⚡

Hazardous Energy

Moving parts, agitators, pressure or electrical energy must be isolated.

🚪

Difficult Rescue

Restricted access can make evacuation and rescue significantly more difficult.

Before Entry

1. Permit to Work

Entry must be authorised through the appropriate permit system with clear conditions and controls.

2. Isolation

Isolate energy sources, lines, valves and other hazards using an approved procedure.

3. Atmospheric Testing

Test oxygen levels, flammable gases and toxic substances before entry and during the work as required.

4. Ventilation

Use suitable ventilation where required.

5. Attendant

The attendant remains outside, monitors the situation and maintains communication with the workers inside.

6. Rescue Plan

A rescue plan must be in place before entry, not after an emergency occurs.

Critical: An unplanned rescue attempt in a confined space can create a second casualty. Do not enter for rescue without the required training, equipment and rescue procedure.

The Role of the Attendant

The attendant does not enter the confined space. They remain outside, maintain communication, monitor conditions and activate the emergency response procedure when required.

Safe practice: The attendant must know who is inside, how long they have been inside, maintain communication and know how emergency assistance will be activated.

Key Takeaways

Never enter a confined space without an approved procedure.

The atmosphere may be hazardous even when the space appears clean.

Atmospheric testing and ventilation are critical controls.

The attendant remains outside and monitors the entry.

Rescue must be planned before entry.
