It is not enough to identify a hazard. We must understand how serious the risk is and which control measures are most effective.

What is Risk Assessment?

Risk assessment is the process of examining a hazard, estimating how likely it is to cause harm and how severe the consequences could be.

Simple idea: Risk = Likelihood × Severity.

Likelihood and Severity

🎲

Likelihood

How likely is it that something unwanted could happen?

Example: A cable across a walkway used by many workers has an increased likelihood of causing a fall.

🚑

Severity

How serious could the consequences be?

Example: A fall may cause anything from a minor injury to a serious fracture.

Hierarchy of Controls

When controlling a hazard, we do not start with PPE. First, we check whether the hazard can be removed or reduced at the source.

1. Elimination

Completely remove the hazard.

Example: Remove an obstruction from a walkway.

2. Substitution

Replace something hazardous with something less hazardous.

Example:We useless hazardous chemicals.

3. Engineering Controls

Use physical or mechanical protection methods.

Example: Machine guards, ventilation systems, barriers.

4. Administrative Controls

Use procedures, training, signs, permits and safe systems of work.

Example: LOTO procedure or safe work instruction.

5. PPE

Use Personal Protective Equipment as the final level of protection.

Example: Gloves, helmet, safety glasses, hearing protection.

Important: PPE does not remove the hazard. It only reduces the worker’s exposure.

Key Takeaways

Risk is linked to likelihood and severity.

First, try to eliminate the hazard.

Engineering controls are stronger than simple instructions.

PPE is important, but it should not be the only protection.
