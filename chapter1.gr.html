Module 1: Introduction to Safety

Safety in industry is not just a rule. It is a daily choice, a personal responsibility and a key part of professional behaviour.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, the worker will be able to:

🎯Explain why safety concerns every worker.
⚠️Recognise basic unsafe behaviours.
👷Understand the importance of personal responsibility.
🗣️Report hazards, incidents and near misses.

Why is safety important?

In an industrial environment, work activities may involve machinery, electrical energy, chemicals, lifting operations, vehicles, hot work and manual handling. One simple mistake can lead to injury, equipment damage, production downtime or a serious accident.

Key message: Safety does not start with a rule. It starts with the decision to stop, think and act correctly.

What does safety culture mean?

Safety culture means that everyone, from management to the newest worker, behaves in a way that protects life, health and the wellbeing of all people at work.

✅

Safe behaviour

I follow instructions, use PPE, report hazards and stop work when something is not safe.

❌

Unsafe behaviour

I bypass guards, rush the job, ignore hazards or continue working even when I know there is a problem.

The STOP Principle

Before starting any task, use the simple STOP principle:

S — Stop
Stop before you act.
T — Think
Think about what could go wrong.
O — Observe
Observe the workplace, the equipment and the people around you.
P — Proceed
Proceed only when the task is safe.

Practical Scenario

You see a colleague temporarily removing a machine guard to complete a task faster. What do you do?

What should we report?

⚠️

Unsafe conditions

Examples: leaks, damaged guards, blocked walkways, damaged cables.

🚧

Unsafe behaviours

Examples: not using PPE, bypassing procedures, working without authorisation.

🟡

Near Miss

An event that did not cause injury, but had the potential to do so.

Remember: Reporting a hazard is not blaming someone. It is an act of prevention.

Key Takeaways

Safety is everyone’s responsibility.

Stop, think, observe and act correctly.
