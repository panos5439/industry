Module 16: Permit to Work

A Permit to Work is a key control system for high-risk activities. It helps confirm that hazards, isolations, protective measures and responsibilities have been checked before work begins.

Why is a Permit to Work Important?

Some activities involve hazards that cannot be adequately controlled through normal operating procedures alone.

Why it matters: A Permit to Work helps prevent high-risk work from starting before hazards, isolations, protective measures and responsibilities have been confirmed.
Important: A signed permit does not automatically make the work safe. Risk assessment, procedures and control measures are still required and must be followed.

What is a Permit to Work?

A Permit to Work is a formal, documented authorisation for a specific high-risk task, at a specific location, for a defined period of time.

📍

Location

Defines the exact work area, equipment or system involved.

🛠️

Scope of Work

Defines exactly what work is authorised to be carried out.

🔒

Control Measures

Records isolations, PPE and other required controls.

⏱️

Validity Period

Defines the start time, expiry time and any reauthorisation requirements.

Work That May Require a Permit to Work

Permit categories may vary between organisations. Always follow the local Permit to Work procedure and site-specific rules.

🔥 Hot Work

Hot Work

Welding, cutting, grinding or other ignition-producing activities.

🕳️ Confined Space

Confined Space Entry

Entry into tanks, pits, silos or other confined spaces.

⚡ Electrical Work

Electrical Work

Work on or near electrical systems.

🔧 Maintenance

Mechanical Work

Maintenance involving stored energy, pressure or moving parts.

⬆️ Working at Height

Working at Height

Work involving the risk of a person or object falling.

⛏️ Excavation

Excavation

Ground work near underground services.

The Permit to Work Process

1. Planning

Define the task, equipment and location.

2. Hazard Identification

Assess hazards and simultaneous activities.

3. Control Measures

Apply isolations, barriers, checks and PPE.

4. Inspection

Confirm the actual conditions at the worksite.

5. Authorisation

Review and accept the permit conditions.

6. Team Briefing

Conduct a toolbox talk before starting work.

7. Monitoring

Confirm that controls remain effective.

8. Closure

Safely restore the system and formally close the permit.

Before Starting Work

Permit Check

  • The task and location are correct.
  • The permit is approved and valid.
  • The team understands the scope of work.
  • The risk assessment has been reviewed.
  • Required isolations have been applied.
  • Correct PPE is being used.

Worksite Check

  • The work area has been inspected.
  • Barriers and warning signs are in place where required.
  • Escape routes are clear.
  • Required checks have been completed.
  • Emergency arrangements are ready.
  • Simultaneous work activities have been coordinated.

Decision Scenario

A maintenance team has a valid permit to replace a pump. During the work, the team discovers that an additional pipe must be opened. This work is not included in the permit, and the pipe may contain residual pressure.

Permit Validity Check

The team returns after a break. Conditions have changed and the permit expiry time has passed.

Key Takeaways

A Permit to Work is a control process, not just a document.

Isolations must be applied and verified.

The whole work team must understand the permit conditions.

Work must stop when conditions or the scope of work change.

The permit should only be closed once the work area is safe.
