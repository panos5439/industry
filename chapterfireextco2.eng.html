CO₂ Fire Extinguisher

The correct type of fire extinguisher can make a difference within seconds. In this module, we learn about the CO₂ fire extinguisher, when it is used, how it extinguishes fire and what hazards may result from incorrect use.

Learning Objectives

🧯 Understand what a CO₂ fire extinguisher is and how it works.
🔥 Recognise which types of fire it is suitable for.
⚡ Understand why it is used on electrical and electronic equipment.
⚠️ Recognise the main hazards associated with incorrect use.
🎯 Select the appropriate type of extinguisher according to the type of fire.

1. What Is a CO₂ Fire Extinguisher?

A CO₂ fire extinguisher contains carbon dioxide in liquefied form, under high pressure.

When activated, the CO₂ is discharged as a white cloud at a very low temperature.

Indicative illustration of a CO₂ fire extinguisher and the way the agent is discharged.

2. Which Fires Is It Suitable For?

🔥

Class B

Suitable for flammable liquids, such as oils, petrol, solvents and similar materials.

⚡

Electrical Equipment

Used on fires involving electrical equipment, such as electrical panels, servers and computers.

Not suitable: for Class A solid fuels, such as wood, paper and textiles, or for Class D metal fires.
Typical application areas: electrical panels, computer rooms, lift machinery rooms, workshops and industrial areas with electrical installations.

3. How Does It Extinguish Fire?

1
DISCHARGE CO₂ is released from the extinguisher.
2
DISPLACEMENT It displaces oxygen around the flame.
3
COOLING The gas is discharged at a very low temperature.
4
EXTINGUISHING The fire is “smothered” without leaving residue.
Unlike powder or foam extinguishers, CO₂ does not leave residue after discharge.

4. Advantages of CO₂

✨

Leaves No Residue

It is particularly useful for electronic and electrical equipment.

⚡

Non-Conductive

The module material presents CO₂ as suitable for use on electrical equipment.

⏱️

Fast Extinguishing

It can provide rapid extinguishing when used correctly.

5. Disadvantages & Hazards

🫁

Asphyxiation Risk

In small enclosed spaces, reduction of the available oxygen may create a serious hazard.

❄️

Very Low Temperature

The nozzle and discharge horn can become extremely cold, creating a risk of frostbite.

⏳

Short Discharge Time

The extinguisher empties relatively quickly, so the available discharge time is limited.

🪵

Not for Solid Fuels

It is not considered effective for solid fuel fires, such as paper and wood.

6. Typical Capacities & Applications

2️⃣

2 kg CO₂

For small electrical panels and small offices.

5️⃣

5 kg CO₂

Common in commercial areas and machinery rooms.

In larger facilities, fixed CO₂ systems may also be installed, activated automatically or manually to protect an entire room.
Key message: Use CO₂ only where it is suitable: on flammable liquids and electrical equipment.
