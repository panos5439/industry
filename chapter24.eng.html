Module 24: Forklifts & Mixed Workplace Traffic

When pedestrians, forklifts, trucks and other mobile equipment share the same workplace, a small mistake can have serious consequences. Safe traffic management requires separation, visibility, communication and predictable behaviour.

Learning Objectives

⚠️ Recognise the main hazards associated with forklifts and mixed workplace traffic.
🚶 Apply safe behaviour as a pedestrian in vehicle movement areas.
🚜 Understand basic principles of safe forklift operation.
📦 Recognise how a load affects stability and visibility.
👁️ Understand the importance of blind spots, intersections and visual confirmation.

24.1 Why Is Mixed Traffic High Risk?

A forklift does not behave like a passenger car. Its weight, load distribution, restricted visibility, turning characteristics and workplace environment create specific hazards.

🚶

Pedestrian Struck by Vehicle

A pedestrian may enter the path of a vehicle without being seen.

↩️

Turning & Rear Swing

When turning, the rear of the forklift may move towards pedestrians, equipment or structures.

👁️

Restricted Visibility

Loads, columns, racking and other equipment can create blind spots.

📦

Falling Load

An unstable, damaged or incorrectly positioned load may shift or fall.

⚖️

Tip-Over

Speed, sharp turns, uneven surfaces or incorrect load handling can affect vehicle stability.

Critical point: Never assume that the operator has seen you. Being physically close to a forklift does not mean that you are within the operator's field of vision.

24.2 The Basic Logic of Safe Traffic

1
SEPARATE Separate pedestrians and vehicles wherever reasonably practicable.
2
LOOK Check the route, blind spots and intersections.
3
COMMUNICATE Make sure the pedestrian and operator are aware of each other.
4
KEEP DISTANCE Do not unnecessarily enter the operating zone of a vehicle.
5
DO NOT ASSUME Proceed only when it is clear that movement can be made safely.

24.3 Safe Pedestrian Behaviour

🚶

Use Designated Routes

Use designated pedestrian walkways, crossings and protected areas where provided.

👀

Visual Confirmation

Before crossing a vehicle route, make sure the operator is aware of your presence.

🛑

Stop at Intersections

Do not appear suddenly from behind doors, racking, columns or stored materials.

↔️

Keep Your Distance

Do not walk too close to a moving forklift and do not stand within its turning zone.

Pedestrian does not mean automatic right of way without checking. Even where a designated crossing is provided, the pedestrian must check that the vehicle has stopped and that it is safe to cross.

24.4 Basic Principles for the Operator

A forklift should only be operated by persons who have the required training, authorisation and competence in accordance with site procedures and applicable requirements.

🔍

Pre-Use Inspection

Before operation, carry out the required inspection of the vehicle and its safety systems.

🐢

Appropriate Speed

Adjust speed to workplace conditions, visibility, traffic and the load.

📣

Intersections

Approach blind corners and intersections with increased caution and use the required warning methods.

👷

Watch for Pedestrians

Reduce speed and stop whenever there is uncertainty about a pedestrian's intention or movement.

24.5 Load, Stability & Visibility

⚖️

Stability

The load affects the centre of gravity and the behaviour of the forklift.

📦

Safe Load

The load must be suitable, stable and correctly positioned before movement.

👁️

Visibility

If the load restricts visibility, follow the designated safe method of travel for the specific workplace and equipment.

⬇️

Travel

During movement, keep the load in the position required by the safe operating method for the equipment.

Never stand under an elevated load. Pedestrians must not stand or walk beneath an elevated load or raised load-handling equipment.

24.6 Blind Spots & Intersections

Many serious incidents do not occur because someone ignored an obvious hazard, but because the pedestrian and operator could not see each other in time.

🏗️

Racking & Columns

These can hide pedestrians and vehicles until the last moment.

🚪

Doors & Exits

A pedestrian may step directly into a vehicle route.

📦

High Load

The load can significantly restrict the operator's field of vision.

🔄

Reversing & Turning

Direction of travel and rear swing require continuous monitoring of the surrounding area.

24.7 Passengers, Parking & Leaving the Vehicle

🚫

Unauthorised Passengers

Do not carry passengers unless the equipment is designed and approved for that specific purpose.

🅿️

Safe Parking

Park the vehicle only in an appropriate location and secure it according to the required procedure.

🔑

Unauthorised Use

Equipment must be protected against use by unauthorised persons.

24.8 Traffic Safety Is a Shared Responsibility

👷

Operators

Safe speed, continuous observation, load control and attention to pedestrians.

🚶

Pedestrians

Designated routes, safe distances and confirmation before crossing.

🏭

Workplace Organisation

Suitable layout, traffic separation, signage, visibility and control of high-risk points.

📢

Hazard Reporting

Blind spots, damaged walkways, obstructions and near misses should be reported.

Goal: Pedestrians and operators should not rely on the assumption that “the other person will notice”. The traffic system should make safe behaviour clear and predictable.

Key Takeaways

🚶
Use designated pedestrian routes and do not enter vehicle routes unpredictably.
👁️
Never assume the operator has seen you. Confirm that crossing is safe.
🚜
Operators must adapt movement to visibility, pedestrians, the load and workplace conditions.
📦
The load affects both stability and forklift visibility.
🛑
When uncertain, stop. Safe movement is more important than speed of work.
